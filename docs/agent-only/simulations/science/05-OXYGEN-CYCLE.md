# Oxygen cycle

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Proposed simulation concept.

## Concept

How does oxygen move between living organisms, the atmosphere, and water?

The simulation presents a pond and its surrounding vegetation, with an underwater view showing aquatic plants, algae, animals, and decomposers. Learners follow oxygen through photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, and exchange between the water and atmosphere. Selectable arrows explain which processes produce molecular oxygen, consume it, or move it between stores.

The central experience is a day-and-night comparison. During daylight, photosynthesis can add oxygen to the pond while respiration continues. At night, photosynthesis stops in this teaching scenario, but plants, animals, and decomposers continue respiring. Learners observe how dissolved oxygen changes across the cycle and why plants should not be portrayed as organisms that only release oxygen.

Learners can change light availability, aquatic plant abundance, water temperature, or the amount of dead organic material. Adding dead material increases oxygen demand as microbes decompose it. A warmer-water comparison should distinguish reduced oxygen solubility from changes in biological activity; the scenario must state which effects it models. An optional mixing control shows how water movement can affect exchange with the atmosphere.

Use a selectable tracer to illustrate an oxygen pathway, alongside explicit stores of atmospheric and dissolved molecular oxygen. Explain that oxygen atoms also occur in water, carbon dioxide, and organic matter: consuming molecular oxygen does not destroy oxygen atoms. The first version focuses on biological oxygen exchange rather than every geological or atmospheric pathway.

## Core investigations

1. Compare dissolved oxygen during daylight and darkness with the same starting ecosystem.
2. Increase aquatic vegetation and observe both daytime production and nighttime respiration.
3. Add dead organic material and trace the resulting oxygen demand from decomposition.
4. Compare cooler and warmer water under the stated model assumptions.
5. Change mixing and observe how exchange with the atmosphere affects the pond.

## Learning goals

- Distinguish oxygen production, consumption, and physical exchange.
- Explain that plants perform respiration as well as photosynthesis.
- Connect decomposition to dissolved oxygen demand.
- Distinguish atmospheric oxygen from the oxygen available to aquatic organisms.
- Recognize that oxygen atoms are conserved as they move among chemical forms.

## Model boundaries

Use finite oxygen stores, bounded process rates, and an explicit account of sources, sinks, and boundary exchanges. Tracers illustrate pathways; they do not replace the store quantities. Show oxygen stress as a qualitative response of fictional aquatic organisms, without presenting the model as a forecast for a real pond. Local changes in pond oxygen should not imply an equally rapid change in the global atmosphere.
