# Layers of the atmosphere and the ozone layer

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Research brief; first-version lesson implemented. See the [implementation and verification report](../../implementation/027-validation/REPORT.md) for shipped scope and actual checks.
Recommended presentation: **2D vertical altitude explorer with an ozone close-up.**

## Concept and learning goals

What changes as we move upward through Earth's atmosphere, and where does ozone protect us?

Learners identify five main atmospheric layers, connect altitude to changing conditions, and locate the ozone-rich region within the stratosphere. They should distinguish ultraviolet protection from the greenhouse effect.

## How it works

The commonly named layers are the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, and exosphere. Almost all ordinary weather occurs in the troposphere. The stratosphere contains the ozone layer; many meteors ablate in the mesosphere; auroras occur at upper-atmosphere altitudes. The ionosphere overlaps several layers rather than forming an extra step in this sequence. Boundaries vary and are not physical walls. [NASA — Earth's Atmospheric Layers](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/earths-atmospheric-layers-3/)

Ozone is a molecule containing three oxygen atoms. Stratospheric ozone absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation, especially UV-B, while oxygen and ozone strongly absorb UV-C. Ground-level ozone is a pollutant. Ozone-depleting substances can release chlorine or bromine that destroy stratospheric ozone; depletion means reduced concentration, not a literal open hole in the air. [EPA — Basic Ozone Layer Science](https://www.epa.gov/ozone-layer-protection/basic-ozone-layer-science)

## How we present it

Begin at ground level and drag an altitude probe upward. Keep the five layers labeled beside a vertical ruler. Show a temperature profile and a relative air-density indicator beside the probe. Select a layer to inspect one characteristic and an example object.

| Teaching band | Approximate altitude guide | Visual cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Troposphere | Surface to roughly 8–15 km | Clouds and weather |
| Stratosphere | Tropopause to about 50 km | Ozone-rich region |
| Mesosphere | About 50–85 km | Meteor trail |
| Thermosphere | About 85–600 km; variable | Aurora |
| Exosphere | Above a variable transition near 600 km | Gradual thinning into space |

These are rounded guides drawn from NASA, not fixed universal boundaries. Use an expanded lower-atmosphere view and a compressed upper-atmosphere view, each with a labeled scale. Show ozone as a concentration gradient within the stratosphere, not a solid shell. [NASA — Earth's Atmospheric Layers](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/earths-atmospheric-layers-3/)

The ozone close-up uses labeled UV-A, UV-B, and UV-C arrows. Compare a normal schematic ozone profile with a depleted one and highlight changes in UV transmission. A separate visible-light arrow avoids suggesting that ozone blocks all sunlight.

## Objects, controls, and observations

- Objects: layer bands, altitude probe, temperature trace, density indicator, ozone profile, and radiation paths.
- Controls: probe altitude; layer inspection; lower/upper scale; ionosphere overlay; normal/depleted ozone preset; next explanation stage.
- Observations: selected layer, approximate altitude, temperature trend, density trend, and qualitative UV transmission.
- Investigation: “Where is the ozone-rich region? Change its concentration: which radiation paths change, and does a gap appear in the atmosphere?”

## First-version model and boundaries

Use a representative atmospheric profile and wavelength-specific qualitative transmission presets. Label profiles as illustrative rather than location-specific measurements. Do not let depletion change every layer's thickness. Do not confuse thermospheric particle temperature with the heating experience in dense air.

Acceptance: layer order remains correct; ozone sits within the stratosphere; ionosphere overlay overlaps layers; UV categories remain distinct; the altitude probe reports physical altitude even when display spacing is compressed.

## Why 2D

Altitude and concentration are the meaningful axes. A vertical section makes them easy to compare and leaves room for labels. A 3D globe can provide context later, but concentric transparent shells would add occlusion without improving this lesson.
