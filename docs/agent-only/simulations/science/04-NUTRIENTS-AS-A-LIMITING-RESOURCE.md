# Nutrients as a limiting resource

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Proposed simulation concept.

## Concept

Will a plant keep growing faster whenever more nutrients are added?

The simulation follows a plant growing under otherwise suitable light and water conditions. Learners adjust the availability of several essential nutrients and observe growth over time. The plant’s current limiting resource is the one whose shortage most strongly constrains growth.

The key experience is a controlled comparison. A nutrient-deficient plant initially responds when the missing nutrient is added, but growth eventually stops increasing because another requirement becomes limiting or because the plant has reached the scenario’s growth ceiling. Adding more of a nutrient that is already sufficient produces little or no additional growth in the model.

Learners can compare balanced nutrition with several kinds of shortage, inspect visible deficiency cues, and follow biomass rather than judging growth only by height. A later exploration can introduce co-limitation, where changing one nutrient alone has little effect because another remains scarce.

The concept should avoid teaching that fertilizer always improves real plant growth. Different species and soils have different requirements, nutrient excess can harm plants and ecosystems, and real growth also depends on water, light, temperature, roots, soil conditions, disease, and competition. The simulation represents a bounded teaching scenario, not fertilizer advice or a crop-yield prediction.

## Learning goals

- Explain what it means for a resource to limit growth.
- Predict when adding a nutrient will increase growth and when it will not.
- Recognize that the limiting resource can change during an experiment.
- Distinguish balanced nutrient availability from simply maximizing every input.
- Compare plant height, biomass, and deficiency signs as different observations.
