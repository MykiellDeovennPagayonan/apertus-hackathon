# Wildfire spread and prescribed burning

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Proposed simulation concept.

## Concept

How can an earlier, carefully controlled burn change the behavior of a later wildfire?

The simulation follows a fire across a landscape made of connected patches of grass, shrubs, trees, and previously burned ground. Wind, fuel moisture, and the amount and continuity of vegetation influence where the fire spreads and how intensely each patch burns.

Learners first observe a wildfire crossing an untreated landscape. They then compare the same ignition and weather conditions after a prescribed burn has reduced fuel in a selected area. The treated area may slow the fire, lower its intensity, break a continuous path of fuel, or make the later fire easier to contain. It should not be presented as a barrier that always stops fire: embers, extreme weather, remaining fuel, and the placement and age of a treatment can change the result.

The central comparison is between **suppressing every fire and allowing fuel to accumulate** versus **using planned fire under suitable conditions to reduce later fuel**. A useful exploration asks learners to change the position, width, and timing of the treatment and then compare total area burned, fire intensity, rate of spread, and whether the fire reaches a community or other protected area.

The simulation should also show that fire is a natural and sometimes necessary ecological process in many landscapes. Prescribed burning is performed by qualified professionals under an approved plan and appropriate weather and fuel conditions; the activity is not guidance for conducting a real burn.

## Learning goals

- Recognize vegetation as fuel whose amount, type, moisture, and continuity affect fire behavior.
- Explain how prescribed burning can reduce the severity or spread of a later wildfire without guaranteeing that it stops.
- Distinguish a planned prescribed fire from an uncontrolled wildfire.
- Compare outcomes under the same ignition and weather conditions.
- Understand that the effectiveness and ecological effects of treatment depend on the ecosystem, conditions, placement, and time since treatment.
