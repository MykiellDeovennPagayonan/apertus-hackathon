# How lightning forms

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Research brief; first-version lesson implemented. See the [implementation and verification report](../../implementation/027-validation/REPORT.md) for shipped scope and actual checks.
Recommended presentation: **2D thundercloud cutaway with a slowed discharge sequence.**

## Concept and learning goals

What happens inside a thundercloud before we see a flash?

Learners connect particle collisions and charge separation to an electrical discharge. They should distinguish leader growth from the bright return stroke and understand that lightning can occur within clouds as well as between cloud and ground.

## How it works

Within a thunderstorm, ice crystals, supercooled water droplets, and graupel interact. In a common teaching pattern, collisions transfer charge, while updrafts carry smaller ice particles upward and denser graupel remains lower or falls. Positive charge accumulates higher in the cloud and negative charge lower down; real charge structures can be more complex. This separation produces strong electric fields and influences charges at the ground. Details of charging and initiation remain active research topics. [NWS — Thunderstorm Electrification](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-electrification)

For a typical negative cloud-to-ground flash, a branched stepped leader develops downward and connects with an upward streamer. A bright return-stroke front then propagates upward along the established channel. That upward propagation should be distinguished from the downward movement of negative charge. Later strokes may reuse the channel. [NWS — Return Stroke](https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-science-return-stroke)

Rapid channel heating causes air expansion and thunder. [NWS — What is Lightning?](https://www.weather.gov/mlb/what_is_lightning) Light reaches a distant observer before sound. [USGS — The Science of Earthquakes, wave-arrival comparison](https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science-earthquakes)

## How we present it

1. Open a cloud cutaway with updraft arrows and individually inspectable ice and graupel tracers.
2. Pause on a collision and reveal charge transfer with plus/minus labels.
3. Follow the tracers into different cloud regions and show a schematic electric-field overlay.
4. Switch to a magnified slow-motion discharge: stepped leader, streamer connection, return stroke, optional repeat stroke.
5. Replay at a visually comfortable speed and show a separate thunder-arrival marker at an observer.

Use the cloud interior for explanation and a compact exterior inset for context. Offer an intracloud preset after the ground-flash sequence. A visible lesson-stage clock prevents the slow animation being mistaken for the real event duration.

## Objects, controls, and observations

- Objects: cloud, ice particles, graupel, updraft, charge regions, ground, leader branches, streamer, and discharge channel.
- Controls: charging-stage step; updraft teaching preset; charge and field overlays; ground/intracloud scenario; discharge slow motion; observer distance; reset.
- Observations: charge separation, selected stage, channel connection, propagation direction, and flash-versus-thunder arrival.
- Investigation: “Before connection, which channel grows downward? After connection, which way does the bright return-stroke front move?”

## First-version model and boundaries

Use staged particle interactions and a documented charge-separation rule, followed by a prescribed branched channel sequence. Keep tracer charges consistent with recorded transfers. Label the field overlay and initiation threshold schematic; do not imply that a simple universal threshold predicts real strike locations.

Avoid the misconception that two whole clouds must collide or rub together to make lightning. The default negative ground-flash sequence is one example, not every lightning type. Updraft strength alone must not determine an exact flash rate. Audio is optional, with a visual thunder cue; use a reduced-flash presentation instead of rapid screen-wide blinking.

Acceptance: charging precedes discharge; leader and streamer connect before the return stroke; the return front follows the existing channel upward; the intracloud preset does not invent a ground connection; the slower sound cue arrives later for more distant observers.

## Why 2D

A cutaway keeps charge regions and channel stages visible together. A 3D cloud would obscure the interior and add costly transparency without making this sequence easier to understand. Depth can be suggested with restrained illustration.
