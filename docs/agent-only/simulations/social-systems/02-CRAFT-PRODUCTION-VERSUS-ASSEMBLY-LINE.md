# Craft production versus an assembly line

Date: 2026-10-10  
Status: Proposed simulation concept.

## Concept

Why can a factory produce more items when each worker performs only one part of the job?

Learners compare two factories making the same product with the same workers and working time. In the first, each worker builds one complete item from beginning to end. In the second, workers specialize in stages and pass partially completed items along an assembly line.

The scene makes the organization of work visible. Learners can follow an individual product, see workers repeat tasks, and watch unfinished items collect between stages. At the end of a run, they compare completed products, time per product, idle time, unfinished work, and defects.

The assembly line should not automatically win. Specialization and overlapping work can increase output, but the entire line can be constrained by its slowest stage. A breakdown may stop later stages, producing too quickly at one station may create a pile of unfinished work, and monotonous repetition may increase fatigue or defects. Craft production may produce fewer items while allowing one worker to control the full process and adapt more easily to varied products.

The main investigation changes how work is divided while keeping the product, workforce, and total time constant. Later comparisons can introduce uneven task times, worker learning, breaks, product variety, or a temporary station failure.

## Learning goals

- Explain division of labor and specialization.
- Distinguish individual completion time from total factory output.
- Identify bottlenecks, idle time, and work accumulating between stages.
- Compare productivity with flexibility, worker experience, and quality.
- Understand that production outcomes depend on how people and tasks are coordinated.
